Seven utility functions, three questions each

The same seven functions run through the whole sheet: differentiate them, decide where they are concave, then maximise them on a budget line. Doing it that way round is deliberate — the concavity answer is what tells you whether the first-order condition you write in part three has found a maximum, a minimum, or nothing at all. Everything here is drawn for N = 2 so it fits on a page, with the general-N formulas alongside.

Convexity and concavity

Two different questions live here, and the problem is really asking both. Concavity is about the Hessian: is it negative semi-definite? Quasiconcavity is about the level sets: is the shaded upper contour set {x : u(x) ≥ u(x⁰)} convex? The second is weaker, survives any increasing relabelling of utility, and is what the consumer problem actually needs.

Reading the chord

The dashed chord joins two points on the indifference curve through your point. If the chord lies inside the shaded region, the upper contour set passed that test; if it cuts outside, the set is not convex and u is not quasiconcave. One chord cannot prove convexity — but one bad chord disproves it, which is all a counterexample needs. Try it on u₄, and on u₅ with ρ > 1.

Where a definiteness verdict reads "semi". Four of the seven have a singular Hessian, each for its own reason: u₁, u₃ and u₄ are piecewise affine, so the Hessian is the zero matrix; u₅ is homogeneous of degree one, which forces Hx = 0; u₆ is linear in x₂, so that direction has no curvature. Only u₂ with Σaᵢ < 1 and u₇ are strictly concave. "Weakly" in the question is doing real work.

Why the extremum is a maximum — four different arguments

Concave objectiveu₂ with Σa ≤ 1, u₃, u₅ with ρ < 1, u₆ with v″ ≤ 0, u₇Concave objective on a convex set ⇒ any stationary point is a global maximum. One line, no second-order condition at the point.

Convex objectiveu₄, u₅ with ρ > 1, u₆ with v″ > 0A convex function on a convex compact set is maximised at an extreme point, so check the N axis intercepts. Any interior stationary point is the minimum.

A gap in the question worth flagging in your answer. The constraint as written is Σpᵢxᵢ = m with no sign restriction on xᵢ. For u₁ and u₄ the problem then has no maximum: send one quantity to +∞ and another to −∞ along the budget line and utility grows without bound. The intended problem carries xᵢ ≥ 0, which is exactly what turns those two into corner solutions. And for u₇ the equality — rather than ≤ — is what allows a negative multiplier and the odd conclusion that extra income can hurt.

Three habits this sheet is trying to build

Check differentiability before differentiating. Two of the seven functions are not differentiable everywhere, and the sheet says so. The right response is to find the bad set, give the derivative off it, and give one-sided derivatives on it.

Decide the curvature before interpreting the FOC. The first-order condition is the same calculation whether the answer is a maximum or a minimum. u₅ makes this concrete: one parameter crossing 1 turns the identical tangency from the best point on the budget line into the worst.

Expect corners, and know what produces them. Non-convex preferences (u₄, u₅ with ρ > 1) push you to the edges; perfect substitutability (u₁) makes the edges tie with everything else only in the knife-edge case; and binding non-negativity (u₃ with a large bᵢ, u₆ with small m) produces them even when preferences are perfectly well behaved.
